# Novena for Relief from Debt and Financial Hardship: Scripture sources

All texts are NABRE (New American Bible, revised edition), checked word for word on 2026-11-21 against bible.usccb.org.

**How they were checked.** Each chapter page was read with WebFetch (one fetch per chapter). The fetch tool will not quote more than about 125 characters at once, so the longer passages were checked by comparing the exact text below against the page character by character: Psalm 128:1–3 (verses 1–2 and verse 3 in two comparisons) and the last sentence of Luke 1:38. Each came back as an exact match. Footnote letters and asterisks are left out. The divine name is printed "LORD" as on the page; the site shows it in small caps.

**Line breaks.** " / " marks a poetry line break as printed on bible.usccb.org (Psalm 128:1–3, Lamentations 3:25–26). The USCCB terms ask for poetic line breaks to be kept.

## Numbering and range changes (RSV-CE → NABRE)

- All references keep their numbers. Psalm 128's title ("A song of ascents.") is printed inside verse 1 in the NABRE, so the numbering does not shift; the title is not quoted.
- **Psalm 128:1–2 → Psalm 128:1–3.** In the NABRE, verse 2 ends with a colon ("you will be blessed and prosper:"), so the sentence runs on into verse 3. Verse 3 was added so the passage ends at a full stop. It keeps the day's theme (the fruit of honest work) and adds the blessing on the household.

## Range checks

- No passage starts or ends in the middle of a quotation, and each ends at the end of a sentence.
  - Matthew 6:31–32 and 6:33 sit inside the Sermon on the Mount; the NABRE opens paragraphs of the sermon with a quote mark, but neither passage starts a paragraph, so no double quote mark dangles. The single quotes in 6:31 open and close within the verse.
  - Luke 1:38 closes Mary's words and ends with a narrative sentence.
  - Lamentations 3:25–26 starts a new sentence at verse 25 ("The LORD is good…") and ends with a full stop. The capital "It" after a semicolon is as printed.
  - Luke 14:28 is a complete question; Jesus' words go on in verse 29, but this verse carries no quotation marks of its own.
  - Matthew 6:33 keeps the NABRE's brackets: "[of God]".
- The epigraph has 11 words and the closing verse 19, within "up to about 25".
- Text quoted in reflections, prayers and steps comes from verified NABRE text:
  - "nothing will be impossible for God." (Luke 1:37, Day 2 reflection; the verse reads "for nothing will be impossible for God.”", checked on https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/1). It was "with God nothing will be impossible".
  - "May it be done to me" (Luke 1:38, Day 2 more). It was "let it be to me".
  - "of little faith" (Matthew 6:30, Day 1 more: "will he not much more provide for you, O you of little faith?", checked on https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/6).
  - "surpasses all understanding" (Philippians 4:7, Day 7 more). It was "passes all understanding".
  - John 14:22 is now paraphrased without quote marks: "Jude (not Judas Iscariot)". It was "not Iscariot" in quote marks; John 14 was not fetched.
  - Matthew 6:24 ("You cannot serve God and mammon.") is paraphrased without quote marks on Day 8: "we cannot serve God and money".
- Echoes of the old RSV wording were brought into line with the NABRE: Day 1 prayer "lay our worries" (was "anxieties", echoing the RSV epigraph); Day 3 "The psalm calls us blessed when we enjoy what our hands provide" (was "a blessing to eat the fruit of the labor of our hands"); Day 4 "calculates the cost" (was "counts"); Day 5 "To hope in silence for the Lord" (was "To wait quietly"); Day 6 "put on love, the bond of perfection, and … let the peace of Christ control our hearts" (was "which binds everything together", "rule in"); Day 7 "prayer and petition, with thanksgiving" (was "supplication"), "it guards our hearts" (was "keeps"); Day 8 prayer "owe nothing to anyone, except love" (was "owe no one anything").

## Saint facts and Church teaching referred to

This novena had no sources file before 2026-11-21. The facts below are listed as they appear in the text; they were not re-checked in this Scripture pass.

- St. Joseph: carpenter of Nazareth, foster father of Jesus; the flight into Egypt (Matthew 2:13–14); feast of St. Joseph the Worker on May 1.
- St. Rita of Cascia (1381–1457, feast May 22): wife, mother, widow and Augustinian nun; family feuds, the loss of her husband and sons; by tradition refused entry to the convent at Cascia three times, a peacemaker who forgave those who had wronged her family.
- St. Jude Thaddeus (feast Oct 28, with St. Simon): apostle, traditionally linked to the Letter of Jude; asks Jesus a question at the Last Supper (John 14:22); long invoked in desperate cases.
- Prudence as a cardinal virtue; paying what we owe as a matter of justice (Day 4).
- Warning against "5-6" loans, high-interest lending apps, gambling, get-rich schemes, and any message that promises riches in return for faith or offerings (Days 5 and 8).
