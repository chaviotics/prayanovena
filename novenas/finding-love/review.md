# Review: Novena for Finding Love (draft)

Reviewed the file as it stood on 2026-11-22. The draft is sound, warm and careful, and close to ready. There are no doctrinal errors. All lengths are within limits (opening 103, closing 33, bios 34 and 32; refl 76–85, more 66–72, prayers 70–78, steps 16–28). Spelling (US), capitals (You/Your/Him for God, "Thank you" to the saints) and the digits/words rule are correct. Day 9's step is the fixed text. Nothing promises a partner, and there is no prosperity framing. Day 2 ("single or married, now and always"), Day 5 ("Not everyone receives such a clear sign, and we should not wait for one"), Day 6 ("A clean heart is not a perfect past") and the tip "If love is slow to come" handle hard cases well. The language about the hoped-for person stays neutral ("someone", "the person", "them"), so nobody is wounded by assumptions. Church teaching is kept through marriage and chastity for all, and the draft does not lecture. The fixes below are one rule breach (a Scripture quote in a bio), two Tobit details, one CCC citation and two pastoral gaps in the tips.

**Confirmed:**
- **Scripture:** all RSV-CE, word for word. Checked against the RSVCE on biblia.com: Tobit 3:16; 1 John 4:16; 1 Corinthians 13:4–7; Philippians 1:9–11; John 2:3–5 (RSVCE has "O woman"). Checked from the RSV OT text: Psalm 91:11, Psalm 51:10, Isaiah 55:8–9, Proverbs 3:5–6, Song of Solomon 8:7 and Jeremiah 29:11. No thee/thou verses. No verse starts or ends inside a quotation.
- **Tobit details, Day 1 and Day 3:** "On the same day", Ecbatana versus Nineveh (3:7); taunted by her father's maids (3:7); seven husbands (3:8), "died in the bridal chamber" (6:13), so "on the wedding night" is fair; prayed by her window (3:11); Raphael sent to help both (3:17); the fish that leaped up at the Tigris (6:2–3); Raphael told Tobias about Sarah (6:10–12); Raphael "communicated the proposal to Raguel" (7:9), so "spoke for him" is correct. Day 6's prayer (8:7: "not … because of lust, but with sincerity … grow old together with her") is paraphrased accurately. Leaving out the demon and the fish-smoke is the right pastoral choice: it keeps the story from reading like a ritual.
- **Sts. Louis and Zélie Martin:** Louis 22 Aug 1823 – 29 Jul 1894; Zélie 23 Dec 1831 – 28 Aug 1877. Louis was refused by the Great St Bernard community because he had not learned Latin. Zélie was refused by the Sisters of St. Vincent de Paul (Daughters of Charity). They met in spring 1858 on the Saint-Léonard bridge in Alençon, where an inner voice told Zélie "This is the one I prepared for you"; Zélie attributed it to Our Lady. They married 13 Jul 1858 at Notre-Dame, Alençon, about three months later. They had nine children, and four died young. Louis sold his watchmaking business in 1870 to join Zélie's lace business. Both were canonized 18 Oct 2015 by Pope Francis, the first couple canonized together. Feast Jul 12. Louis was thirty-four at the wedding (the Carmel archive rounds to 35). Sources: Carmel de Lisieux archives, "Louis before Zélie"; Wikipedia, "Louis Martin and Marie-Azélie Guérin"; Aleteia, 11 Feb 2019. The bridge story has no primary document and is told by the family and in the Carmel's account. The draft frames it "As their family told it" and adds a sentence against waiting for signs. Keep both.
- **St. Raphael:** Feast Sep 29 with Sts. Michael and Gabriel (a Feast on the General Roman Calendar). "Travelers & happy meetings" is a traditional patronage, not an official one. That he is invoked by people hoping to meet a good spouse is long-standing devotion, and it rests on Tobit 6–8.
- **"Mary, Mother of Fair Love"** is a real and fitting title. It comes from Sirach 24:24 in the Vulgate ("Ego mater pulchrae dilectionis"), which St. John Paul II cites in the *Letter to Families* (1994), no. 13: "fairest love (cf. Sir 24:24, Vulg.)". The Church's *Collection of Masses of the Blessed Virgin Mary* has Mass no. 36, "The Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of Fairest Love". "Fair Love" is the Douay-Rheims wording and is common in devotion. See item 8.
- **CCC 1604** ("God who created man out of love also calls him to love — the fundamental and innate vocation of every human being") is paraphrased accurately. **CCC 2337 and 2339** support "integration of sexuality within the person" and "apprenticeship in self-mastery" (vatican.va). On the "every person" sentence, see item 4.
- **Sacraments:** Confession and Mass fit, and Day 6 offers Confession as a fresh start. The online-safety line ("meet in a public place and never send money") is the right warning against romance scams. "A medal or statue is a sign of prayer, not a charm" covers folk shortcuts.
- **Closing verse (Jeremiah 29:11):** it sits after nine days of "Your will be done", so it reads as hope, not as a promise of a spouse. Keep it.

1. **MUST: saints[0].bio (Scripture quoted; journey destination).**
   - "one of the seven holy angels who present the prayers of the saints" is Tobit 12:15 word for word. Scripture outside a day's verse must be paraphrased. The approved Healing novena has the same phrase, so fix it there too.
   - "a long journey to Sarah" is not accurate. Tobias went to recover money left with Gabael in Media and met Sarah on the way.
   - Replace with (34 words): "Archangel of the Book of Tobit, who stands before God and brings Him the prayers of His people. Unrecognized, he guided young Tobias on a long journey, during which Tobias met and married Sarah."

2. **SHOULD: days[8].more (Tobit 12 order).** The draft says Raphael "revealed who he was, and told Tobit and Tobias to praise God and give Him thanks (Tobit 12:6)". The cited command comes first, in 12:6; he says who he is only in 12:15. (A second call to give thanks, in 12:20, does come afterwards, but that is not the verse cited.) Replace with (73 words): "When the journey was over, Raphael told Tobit and Tobias to praise God and give Him thanks (Tobit 12:6). Only then did he tell them who he was. Looking back over these nine days, we may notice small graces: a new friendship, a wiser choice, a calmer heart, a hope renewed. Trusting God’s will does not mean we stop asking. It means we keep praying in peace, knowing that we are loved."

3. **SHOULD: days[5].refl ("not with fear"; no chapter).** Tobias said plainly that he was afraid (Tobit 6:14), and Raphael answered "Do not be afraid" (6:17). "Began not with fear but with prayer" smooths that over, though the fear is the useful part for anxious readers. The passage also has no chapter reference, while the other Tobit passages do. Replace with (90 words): "On their wedding night, Tobias and Sarah rose and prayed together (Tobit 8). Tobias had been afraid, but he trusted God. He told God that he took Sarah as his wife with a sincere heart, not out of lust, and asked that they might grow old together. True love wants the good of the other person, not only what they can give us. It is honest, patient and respectful of body and heart. Today we ask God for a clean heart, and for a love that is pure and free."

4. **SHOULD: days[5].more (CCC citation range).** "The Church calls every person to chastity, married or single" is taught in CCC 2348–2349 ("All the baptized are called to chastity"; "in the way that is suited to their state of life"), not in 2337–2339. Replace with (74 words): "All the baptized are called to chastity, each in the way suited to their state of life (CCC 2348–2349). Chastity is not a fear of love. It integrates our sexuality into the whole person, an apprenticeship in self-mastery that frees us to love (CCC 2337–2339). Where we have fallen short, God’s mercy is greater: Confession gives a fresh start. A clean heart is not a perfect past, but a heart God has made new."

5. **SHOULD: tips[2].p (people who have been hurt; people married before).** The tip speaks kindly to someone after a breakup, but two groups in a general audience are missing:
   - Someone whose past relationship was abusive or controlling needs to hear "get help", not only "take your time".
   - A divorced Catholic hoping to marry again needs to know, gently, to talk with a priest first. The tip should not mention annulment by name or sound like a verdict.
   - Replace with (88 words): "Waiting is not a sign that something is wrong with you, or that you prayed too little. Your worth does not depend on being chosen (Day&nbsp;2). If you are healing from a breakup or a love that was not returned, take your time: God is patient with every heart. If a past relationship hurt you or left you feeling unsafe, talk to someone you trust and get the help you need. If you were married before, speak with a priest about your situation before beginning a new relationship."

6. **CONSIDER: days[1].more (single life, CCC 1658).** "A generous single life" is right. The Catechism has a line that speaks straight to people who are single not by choice, which is this novena's reader. Replace with (75 words): "The Church teaches that God, who created us out of love, also calls us to love: this is the fundamental and innate vocation of every human being (CCC 1604). That call can be lived in marriage, in consecrated life, or as a single person; single people are especially close to Jesus’ heart (CCC 1658). Whatever shape our life takes, love given is never wasted. Today we let God remind us how deeply we are loved."

7. **CONSIDER: tips[1].p (praying for someone else).** Every step assumes you are praying for yourself ("what you hope for in love", "say yes to one invitation", "the person you may one day love"). A parent praying for a grown child is a likely reader. One clause fixes this without rewriting nine steps. Replace with (90 words): "If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days. Pair prayer with action: say yes to good invitations, meet people through your parish, friends and community, and ask someone you trust for honest advice (Day&nbsp;5). With people you meet online, meet in a public place and never send money. A medal or statue is a sign of prayer, not a charm. At “N.”, say the person’s name, or “me” if you pray for yourself; if you pray for someone else, offer each day’s step for them."

8. **CONSIDER: "Mother of Fair Love" or "Fairest Love".** The current title is correct; see Confirmed. The Church's English liturgical book uses "Mother of Fairest Love" (Collection of Masses no. 36), and so does the English *Letter to Families*. Switching would match the liturgy. It would touch the litany, description, ogDescription, openingPrayer, Days 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 9, and the Day 7 step. "Fairest" replaces "Fair" one for one, so no word count changes. If you keep "Fair Love", no change is needed.

9. **CONSIDER: smaller points.**
   - **Titles:** Days 2, 4 and 5 write "Saint John" and "Saint Paul" in the reflections. Every other novena writes "St. Paul" and "St. John". Change to "St." (word counts unchanged).
   - **saints[1].patron:** "First married couple canonized together" is a distinction, not a patronage. It is true and more informative than an invented patronage, so keep it.
   - **days[6].more:** "Louis Martin was thirty-four when he married Zélie" is correct. Readers older than 34 may hear it as "and you're older". It is optional, but the line could go, with "God’s timing is rarely the same as ours, but it is never careless" kept.
   - **credits:** the skill's format ends "Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons." if the images came from Commons. There is no `images.md` in this folder yet, unlike the other novenas.
   - **sources.md:** not yet written. Record the Scripture check (step 4) and add Tobit 3:7–17, 6:2–17, 7:9, 8:4–8, 12:6, 12:15, CCC 1604, 1658, 2337–2339, 2348–2349, the Carmel de Lisieux page and the *Letter to Families* no. 13.
