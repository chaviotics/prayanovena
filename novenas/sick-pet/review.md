# Catholic content review: Novena for a Sick Pet (draft)

Reviewer: independent, one pass (text and pictures). No files edited.
Checked: novena.json (all text), sources.md, images.md, art.json, art/images.md, research.md, contact sheets art.jpg / sheet.jpg, qa.jpg, every day crop and whole picture (converted and viewed one by one), and Commons metadata for 13 files via `tools/art.py meta` (Shields, Tissot, Velázquez, Fra Angelico, Giotto x2, Tintoretto, Dürer, Hicks, Puccio di Simone, Rijksmuseum Martin, plus the full Martin record).

Overall: the doctrine is sound and well balanced. No promise of healing, nothing about pets in heaven, CCC 2416/2418 used fairly (Day 3 keeps "you are worth more than many sparrows", Day 4 and the tips keep cost and people in view), kind to those whose animal dies (Day 8: "never a sign that our faith was weak"), and Day 7 leaves honest room for end-of-life decisions without naming or judging them. Saint dates, feasts and the saints-per-day rule are right (Francis days 2, 3, 8; Anthony 4, 7; Martin 5, 6; all on Day 9). Legends are kept out or marked. Scripture is RSV-CE with no thee/thou. Lengths match habitual-sin.

## SUGGESTIONS

**Text and doctrine**

1. **Room for end-of-life decisions (Day 8 prayer or tips).** Day 7 handles the choice well. But someone who has just had an animal put to sleep may carry guilt, and Day 8 doesn't speak to it. This stays within Church teaching (CCC 2418 condemns *needless* suffering or death; a humane end on a vet's advice isn't forbidden) without endorsing anything:
   - Day 8 prayer, after "hold our sorrow and give us Your peace.": add `If we had to make a hard choice for N.'s sake, free us from guilt that is not ours to carry.`
   - Or add to the tips, "Keeping love in balance", after "...not a lack of love.": `The Church asks us not to make animals suffer or die needlessly (CCC 2418); when nothing more can be done, ending an animal's suffering humanely, on a vet's advice, is not a failure of love.`
2. **Day 8 `more`: "on his deathbed in 1226 he repeated that verse".** The early sources tell it in different ways. The *Assisi Compilation* says he added the stanza on Sister Death when told his end was near, and Celano says he had the brothers sing the Canticle as he died. A safer and well-attested version: `In his Canticle of the Creatures he praised God even for "Sister Death," and as he lay dying in 1226 he asked his brothers to sing it for him.`
3. **Day 4 `more`: a closer fit is available in the same source.** Athanasius (*Life of Antony* 3) says that soon afterwards Anthony heard "Do not be anxious about tomorrow" (Mt 6:34) and gave away even what he had kept for his sister. That is the same chapter as the Day 4 verse. Possible replacement for the second sentence: `At about twenty he heard Jesus tell the rich young man to sell what he had and give to the poor, and soon after heard, "Do not be anxious about tomorrow." St. Athanasius, who knew him, writes that Anthony gave away everything, then lived for many years in the Egyptian desert, praying, weaving mats and tending a small garden.` (Quote Mt 6:34 only after checking it word for word against the RSV-CE, or paraphrase it without quote marks.)
4. **Martin's patron line "Patron of social justice".** It's accurate (Britannica, Franciscan Media), but it says nothing to someone praying for an animal. Other novenas use descriptive lines ("Hermit of the Sahara"), so consider `Friend of the sick, the poor & animals` or `Patron of social justice; friend of animals`. Don't write "patron of animals": that isn't an established patronage. Francis's line ("Patron of animals & ecology") and Anthony's ("Protector of animals") are fine as they stand.
5. **Tips, "Alongside prayer": sacraments.** "go to Confession and attend Mass ... offering them for your intention" reads oddly for an animal. Confession is about one's own soul, and a Mass offered "for" an animal isn't customary. Suggested: `If you can, go to Mass during these 9 days and bring this intention to God there; a good Confession helps us pray with a clear heart.`
6. **description:** "asking healing, comfort and peace" should read `asking for healing, comfort and peace`.
7. **Martin portrait alt:** what looks like a "staff" is a pole with a lantern, and the most telling detail, a basket of bread on his arm (his care for the poor), isn't mentioned. Suggested: `Engraving: St. Martin de Porres in his Dominican habit, with a halo, holding a crucifix, a basket of bread on his arm and a lantern at his side`.

**Pictures**

8. **Day 3 (Sermon to the Birds):** the birds the look line points to ("gathered at Francis's feet") are cut through at the bottom edge, so only their tops show. Moving the crop down a little, e.g. `[0.0, 0.39, 1.0, 0.967]`, would show them whole above the red frame.
9. **Day 4 (Velázquez raven):** the file is already a detail, and no hermit is visible in the crop or the whole picture. The bread-bearing raven is also legend (Jerome's *Life of Paul*, via the *Golden Legend*, as the Commons description says). Suggested alt: `A black raven swoops down past a rock, carrying a round loaf of bread in its beak.` Suggested look: `Look again at the raven bringing the loaf, as the old story of Anthony and Paul the Hermit tells. Where have you seen the Father provide for you lately, in ways small or large?`
10. **Day 7 (Shields) alt:** "with the rest of the flock pressed close around him" isn't visible in the day crop. Consider: `Christ, crowned with thorns, carries two lambs in his arms among the trees.`
11. **Museums (place).** The tool allows them to be left empty, but each is known from the Commons record or the file name. Day 1 `Albertina, Vienna`; Day 2 `Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice`; Day 3 `Upper Basilica of San Francesco, Assisi`; Day 4 `Museo del Prado, Madrid`; Day 7 `Manchester Art Gallery` (accession 1913.17 is in the Commons file name; Commons credit: Art UK); Day 8 `Bardi Chapel, Santa Croce, Florence`.
12. **Days 6 and 7: small sources.** Tissot is 453 px wide and Shields 506 px. Both are acceptable at 720×540. Day 6 is soft but reads well, since Tissot's watercolour hides the softness. Day 7 is the weakest of the nine, with visible JPEG artefacts and glare from a photo of a varnished painting, but it's still clear and honest to Isaiah 40:11. Replace it only if a sharper public-domain Good Shepherd carrying lambs turns up. In Day 6 the crop leaves just the legs of the Samaritan's beast at the top right edge. Harmless, but starting the crop slightly lower would clear them.
13. **Modesty: all nine pass,** in the day crop and the whole picture. The Day 6 wounded man wears a loincloth, as is traditional. The Day 9 whole picture has a child loosely draped in a cloth, which is outside the crop in either version above. Brightness is fine everywhere (Day 2 and Day 9 are the darkest, with mean luminance about 80, still clear).
14. **Credits checked against Commons:** they match for Tissot (1886–94, Brooklyn Museum), Fra Angelico (1438, National Gallery of Art, Washington), Velázquez (title, c. 1634), Giotto Sermon ("Formerly attributed to Giotto", 1297–99), Giotto Bardi (c. 1325), Tintoretto (1551–52), Dürer (1508), Hicks (c. 1846–47, Dallas Museum of Art), Shields ("Frederic Shields", no date) and Puccio di Simone (1353, Pinacoteca Civica, Fabriano).
15. **Martin de Porres portrait (Rijksmuseum RP-P-1913-3934): acceptable.** Commons marks it CC0, and the Rijksmuseum says "Publiek domein", dating it 1852 – ca. 1900 (the credit matches). The museum bought it in 1913, so it was published before then, which makes it public domain in the US. For it to still be in copyright under life+70, Michel Vermeiren would have to have died after 1955, which is implausible for a printmaker active from the mid-19th century. Suggest adding "acquired by the Rijksmuseum in 1913" to images.md as the reasoning.
