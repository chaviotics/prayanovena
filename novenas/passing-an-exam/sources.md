# Novena for Passing an Exam: Scripture sources

All texts are NABRE (New American Bible, revised edition), checked word for word on 2026-11-21 against bible.usccb.org.

**How they were checked.** Each chapter page on bible.usccb.org was fetched once with WebFetch and the verses were read off it line by line, with the page's punctuation, capitals and poetry lines. The divine name is printed "LORD" as on the page. Proverbs 16:3, Psalm 127:2 and James 1:5 are shared with the Work and Livelihood novena and use identical text there.

**Line breaks.** The " / " breaks follow the poetry lines on the USCCB pages (Wisdom 7:7, Psalm 32:8, Isaiah 41:13, Proverbs 16:3, Psalm 127:2, Psalm 25:21, Proverbs 3:5–6). New Testament passages are prose. NABRE capitalizes the first word of some poetry lines even after a comma or semicolon ("To eat bread…", "It is I…", "In all your ways…"); these capitals are kept as printed.

## Numbering and range changes from the RSV-CE version

- **Numbering.** No reference changes. The NABRE prints the psalm titles as part of verse 1 in Psalms 25 ("Of David."), 32 ("Of David. A *maskil*.") and 127 ("A song of ascents. Of Solomon."), so 25:21, 32:8 and 127:2 keep their RSV numbers; this was checked on each chapter page.
- **Ranges.** All ranges are unchanged (old RSV ref = NABRE ref for every passage).
- **printClosing** now splits Psalm 32:8 at the NABRE poetry line.

## Range checks

- No passage starts or ends in the middle of a quotation, and each ends at the end of a sentence.
  - Isaiah 41:13: the NABRE prints God's words ("Do not fear, I will help you.") without quote marks, so none dangle; the verse ends with a full stop.
  - Colossians 3:23–24 is one sentence (v.23 alone ends on a comma). Its NABRE wording ends "be slaves of the Lord Christ", which reads strongly out of the context of vv. 22–25 (counsel to slaves); the reflection takes it as "serving the Lord".
  - John 14:27 sits inside the Last Supper discourse, whose quotation opens at 14:23 and closes at 14:31; verse 27 itself contains no quote marks.
  - Matthew 5:16 begins "Just so," (following on from the lamp of v.15); it is a full sentence with no quote marks.
  - James 1:5 begins "But", as the verse does in the NABRE; it is a full sentence.
  - Proverbs 3:5–6 ends with a full stop; v.5 alone ends on a semicolon.
- Text quoted in reflections and prayers comes from these passages:
  - "Do not fear, I will help you." (Isaiah 41:13, Day 1 reflection; was "Fear not, I will help you").
  - Echoed without quote marks: "entrust our works to the Lord" (Proverbs 16:3, Day 2 reflection and prayer); "generously and ungrudgingly" (James 1:5, Day 3); "from the heart, as for the Lord" (Colossians 3:23, Day 4); "it is vain to put off our rest at night" and "God gives to His beloved even in sleep" (Psalm 127:2, Day 5 reflection and prayer); "integrity and uprightness may preserve" (Psalm 25:21, Day 6); "our light shine before others through good deeds that glorify the Father" (Matthew 5:16, Day 8); "trusting in the Lord with all our heart, not relying on our own intelligence" and "make straight our paths" (Proverbs 3:5–6, Day 9).
  - The Day 5 reflection and prayer used to lean on the RSV "eating the bread of anxious toil; for he gives to his beloved sleep". They now follow the NABRE: it is vain to put off rest at night, and God gives to His beloved in sleep; "anxious toil" in the prayer became "sleepless toil".
- **Permission.** The NABRE may be quoted up to 5,000 words site-wide without written permission, provided no complete book is quoted and the quotations are under 40% of the work. This novena quotes 13 verses, about 260 words of NABRE text (plus a few words echoed in the reflections). No complete book is quoted.

## Saint facts used, and how sure

- **St. Joseph of Cupertino** (1603–1663), feast Sep 18: Franciscan friar and priest from Copertino in Apulia; struggled with learning; known for ecstasies in prayer; canonized 1767. Certain. The diaconate examination story (Day 4) is tradition and is introduced with "The story is told".
- **St. Thomas Aquinas** (c. 1225–1274), feast Jan 28: Dominican; Doctor of the Church; Summa Theologiae; hymns for the Corpus Christi office; patron of Catholic schools (Leo XIII, 1880). Certain.
  - Day 2: "it is said" he never began to study or write without praying (early biographers). The prayer before study traditionally attributed to him ("Creator of all things") is paraphrased, not quoted; its authorship is not certain.
  - Day 5: he taught that the mind needs rest and recreation, retelling the story of St. John and the bow (Summa Theologiae II-II, q. 168, a. 2). Paraphrased.
  - Day 8: after an experience at Mass near the end of his life (Dec 1273) he stopped writing, saying all he had written seemed like straw. Paraphrased, no quotation marks.
- **Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom**: a title from the Litany of Loreto. Certain.

## Self-review (strict Catholic editor pass)

- Nothing promises a pass. Prayers ask for wisdom, diligence, rest, honesty and peace, and leave the result to God's will (opening prayer, closing prayer, Days 2, 6, 9).
- Kindness to those who fail and must retake: Day 9 reflection and "more", and the "Alongside prayer" tip. Day 9 says plainly that a disappointing result is not a sign of weak prayer or of God's abandonment.
- No superstition: Day 7 says a rosary or Hail Mary at the exam is not a lucky charm. Day 6 and the tip warn against leaked questions, copied answers and fixers.
- Sacraments: Confession and Mass (tip), Communion before the exam (tip), Confession for past dishonesty (Day 6).
- Image `alt` texts are placeholders ("Painting: St. …") until the images are chosen.

## Review

An independent Catholic review (review.md) found 1 error and 6 improvements, applied 2026-11-20: the prayer before study is "long attributed to" St. Thomas; the "new school year" line in the tip was cut (every day is about one exam) and replaced with a line on talking to someone when worry or disappointment is too heavy; memory is no longer listed as part of the gift of wisdom; Luke 2:19, 51 for "the events of His childhood"; Day 9 prayer opens "Heavenly Father"; ogDescription names St. Thomas Aquinas; "slow" no longer repeated.

On 2026-11-21 all Scripture was switched from the RSV-CE to the NABRE (owner's decision for the whole site), and the reflections and prayers that echoed RSV wording were adjusted (Days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9).
