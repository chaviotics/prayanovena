# Novena for Passing an Exam: Scripture sources

All texts are RSV-CE. Checked word for word on 2026-11-20 with WebFetch.

**No thee/thou (2026-11-21, Chav).** Psalm 25:21 (Day 6) used "thee/thou/thy"; they were replaced with the RSV-CE verses marked in the table, checked word for word on 2026-11-21 (BibleGateway RSVCE and/or EWTN RSV-CE). Reflections, prayers and steps that echoed the old verses were adjusted. Psalm line breaks follow the standard RSV lineation.

**How they were checked.** Each verse was checked on its BibleGateway **verse page** (`/verse/en/…`), which lists the RSVCE text next to other versions. Multi-verse passages were checked one verse at a time. Old Testament texts were also checked on EWTN's online Bible (labeled RSV-CE), which prints "LORD" in capitals. Where the BibleGateway summary disagreed with EWTN, a third source settled it:
- **Isaiah 41:13.** The BibleGateway summary gave "Do not fear". EWTN (RSV-CE) and biblestudytools.com (RSV) both give "Fear not", so "Fear not" is used.
- **Psalm 25:21.** BibleGateway didn't label which line was the RSVCE. EWTN (RSV-CE) and biblestudytools.com (RSV) both give "May integrity…".

**Line breaks.** Neither source shows poetry line breaks in the fetched text. The " / " breaks in Proverbs 16:3, Psalm 127:2, Psalm 25:21 and Proverbs 3:5–6 follow the standard RSV lineation. The words and punctuation are verified, but the breaks are not. Isaiah 41:13 is given as prose, without breaks.

## Range checks

- No passage starts or ends in the middle of a quotation.
  - Isaiah 41:13 closes its inner quotation.
  - Colossians 3:23–24 is one full sentence (v.23 alone ends on a comma, so both verses are used).
  - John 14:27 and Proverbs 3:5–6 are whole verses ending in full stops.
- Text from these passages that the reflections and prayers quote or echo: "Fear not, I will help you" (Isaiah 41:13, Day 1 reflection), "it is in vain" and "You give Your beloved sleep" (Psalm 127:2, Day 5), "whoever walks in integrity walks securely" (Proverbs 10:9, Day 6), "trusting in the Lord with all our heart, not relying on our own insight" and "make straight our paths" (Proverbs 3:5–6, Day 9). Other Scripture is paraphrased and cited by reference only.
- Permission limits: 13 verses in total. No whole book is quoted, and no book is quoted beyond 25%.

## Saint facts used, and how sure

- **St. Joseph of Cupertino** (1603–1663), feast Sep 18: Franciscan friar and priest from Copertino in Apulia; struggled with learning; known for ecstasies in prayer; canonized 1767. Certain. The diaconate examination story (Day 4) is tradition and is introduced with "The story is told".
- **St. Thomas Aquinas** (c. 1225–1274), feast Jan 28: Dominican; Doctor of the Church; Summa Theologiae; hymns for the Corpus Christi office; patron of Catholic schools (Leo XIII, 1880). Certain.
  - Day 2: "it is said" he never began to study or write without praying (early biographers). The prayer before study traditionally attributed to him ("Creator of all things") is paraphrased, not quoted; its authorship is not certain.
  - Day 5: he taught that the mind needs rest and recreation, retelling the story of St. John and the bow (Summa Theologiae II-II, q. 168, a. 2). Paraphrased.
  - Day 8: after an experience at Mass near the end of his life (Dec 1273) he stopped writing, saying all he had written seemed like straw. Paraphrased, no quotation marks.
- **Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom**: a title from the Litany of Loreto. Certain.

## Self-review (strict Catholic editor pass)

- Nothing promises a pass. Prayers ask for wisdom, diligence, rest, honesty and peace, and leave the result to God's will (opening prayer, closing prayer, Days 2, 6, 9).
- Kindness to those who fail and must retake: Day 9 reflection and "more", and the "Alongside prayer" tip. Day 9 says plainly that a disappointing result is not a sign of weak prayer or of God's abandonment.
- No superstition: Day 7 says a rosary or Hail Mary at the exam is not a lucky charm. Day 6 and the tip warn against leaked questions, copied answers and fixers.
- Sacraments: Confession and Mass (tip), Communion before the exam (tip), Confession for past dishonesty (Day 6).
- Image `alt` texts are placeholders ("Painting: St. …") until the images are chosen.

## Review

An independent Catholic review (review.md) found 1 error and 6 improvements, applied 2026-11-20: the prayer before study is "long attributed to" St. Thomas; the "new school year" line in the tip was cut (every day is about one exam) and replaced with a line on talking to someone when worry or disappointment is too heavy; memory is no longer listed as part of the gift of wisdom; Luke 2:19, 51 for "the events of His childhood"; Day 9 prayer opens "Heavenly Father"; ogDescription names St. Thomas Aquinas; "slow" no longer repeated. Psalm 127:2 and Colossians 3:23–24 were not re-fetched in the review (web budget); they were verified when written.
