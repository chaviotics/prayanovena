## Saints and facts

### Mary, Mother of Divine Providence (litany only, no saint card)
No saint fact is stated in the text. Day 2 uses only the Cana account (John 2:1–11, Scripture). The title's legend (St. Philip Benizi) is not used. For the picture: Scipione Pulzone, *Mater Divinae Providentiae*, c. 1580, honored by the Barnabites at San Carlo ai Catinari, Rome (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Providence).

### St. Joseph (saints[] entry reused from work-and-livelihood; openingRole "who led the Holy Family to safety in Egypt")
- Scripture only: Matthew 2:13–14 (flight into Egypt), used on Day 7 and in the opening prayer; thanked on Day 9. No facts about his life in Egypt. Portrait and credit as in work-and-livelihood (Guido Reni).

### St. Cajetan (saints[] entry reused from work-and-livelihood)
- Dates, feast, Theatines, Naples: https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03145a.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cajetan. HISTORICAL.
- Traditional patron of the unemployed (Day 3 "is invoked by many people looking for work"; review 2026-11-26 dropped "has long been invoked": the patronage is real but largely modern): https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-Cajetan-of-Thiene
- Crowds at San Cayetano, Buenos Aires, every August 7, asking for bread and work (Day 3): as in the work-and-livelihood novena's sources.
- Bank for the poor: dropped after review (2026-11-26). The Naples Monte di Pietà history credits two laymen, the work-and-livelihood review had already cut the claim, and his role is UNCONFIRMED. Day 7 is Scripture only.

### St. John Bosco (saints[] entry reused from talents; openingRole changed to "who went looking for work as a boy")
- About eleven, sent by his mother to look for work as a farmhand; Moglia farm near Moncucco, Feb 1827 to Nov 1829 ("nearly three years"): Memoirs of the Oratory, English edition, editor's note 4 to ch. 5, https://archive.sdb.org/Documenti/Don_Bosco/DB_MO/MO_EN.pdf. HISTORICAL. The "turned away first" story (Memorie biografiche) is not used.
- Lodged with a tailor (John Roberta, Castelnuovo), took up tailoring, was offered the business and declined, "I had other ambitions": Memoirs, same file. Not used in the text after review (it pictured a saint turning down work on the "any honest work" day). His own account ("he later wrote" sense).
- Waiter and bartender as a student in Chieri, to pay his way: Memoirs of the Oratory (Lyons translation), chapter "Waiter and bartender", sdl.sdb.org. Stated as "he later wrote". No blacksmith, no café owner's name.
- Written apprenticeship contracts from 1851 on, fair pay and terms; the November 1851 contract signed by employer, boy, family and Don Bosco: M. Ribotta, https://www.salesian.online/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/2-M.-Ribotta-Training-Boys-to-Earn-a-Living-The-Beginnings-of-Vocational-Education-at-the-Oratory.pdf ; https://infoans.org/en/sections/news/item/14669-italy-don-bosco-s-first-apprenticeship-contract-turns-170-years-old. HISTORICAL. "Oldest surviving" is not claimed.
