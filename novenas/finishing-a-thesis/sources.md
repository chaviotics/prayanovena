## Saints

- **St. Thomas Aquinas** (c. 1225–1274, feast January 28): Dominican; studied under St. Albert the Great in Cologne; taught at the University of Paris; the *Summa Theologiae* is arranged as questions and articles with objections and replies, written from about 1265 until he stopped in 1273. Named patron of Catholic universities, colleges and schools by Leo XIII (*Cum hoc sit*, 1880). Remedies for sorrow: *Summa Theologiae* I-II q.38 (pleasure, tears, the sympathy of friends, contemplation of truth, sleep and baths). The "dumb ox" story comes from his early biographers (William of Tocco) and is told as a story.
- **St. Albert the Great** (c. 1200–1280, feast November 15): Dominican, Bishop of Regensburg (1260–1262), teacher of Thomas in Cologne; canonized and declared a Doctor of the Church by Pius XI in 1931; named patron of natural scientists by Pius XII in 1941. Paraphrase from *De mineralibus* II.ii.1 (natural science is not simply accepting what others say but seeking the causes of things).
- **St. Giuseppe Moscati** (25 July 1880 – 12 April 1927, feast November 16): physician at the Ospedali Riuniti degli Incurabili in Naples, research in physiological chemistry, university teaching; beatified by Paul VI (1975), canonized by John Paul II (25 October 1987). Quotations from his personal notes: "Ama la verità, mostrati qual sei, e senza infingimenti e senza paure e senza riguardi" (rendered in part); "Non la scienza, ma la carità ha trasformato il mondo, in alcuni periodi" (paraphrased).
