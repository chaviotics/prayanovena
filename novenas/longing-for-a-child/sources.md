# Novena for Couples Longing for a Child: sources

## Scripture (RSV-CE)

All texts are RSV-CE, checked word for word on 2026-11-22 against the RSVCE chapter files in the GitHub repository `andrewheekin/obsidian-holy-bible` (headed "(RSVCE)", fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com; BibleGateway rate-limited the workspace). James 1:5, Philippians 4:6–7 and Romans 15:13 were also read on BibleGateway RSVCE pages (header "Revised Standard Version Catholic Edition") and match.

**No thee/thou.** None of these passages has archaic address. 1 Samuel 1:11 (Hannah's vow, "if thou wilt...") was deliberately left out for that reason; Day 1 uses 1:10 only.

**Quotation marks.** 1 Samuel 1:8 carries its own opening and closing quotes. John 15:5 sits inside a speech that opens at 15:1, so the verse alone has no quotation marks. Psalm 62:8 ends with "Selah" in the RSV, left out as a liturgical note (as in the other novenas' psalm verses). Isaiah 25:8 has "Lord GOD" (small caps GOD for the divine name) and "LORD".

## Church teaching (checked on vatican.va, 2026-11-22)

- **CCC 2374:** "Couples who discover that they are sterile suffer greatly." Quoted on Day 2.
- **CCC 2375:** research aimed at reducing human sterility is to be encouraged, at the service of the person. Day 6 ("medical care that looks for and heals the causes").
- **CCC 2376–2377:** techniques involving a third party are gravely immoral; homologous techniques that dissociate the sexual act from the procreative act "remain morally unacceptable". Day 6 names IVF without labels like "sin" or "grave", out of care for readers.
- **CCC 2378:** "A child is not something owed to one, but is a gift."
- **CCC 2379:** "The Gospel shows that physical sterility is not an absolute evil"; adoption and service named. Day 8 quotes the phrase.
- **CCC 1654:** spouses to whom God has not granted children can have a conjugal life full of meaning; their marriage "can radiate a fruitfulness of charity, of hospitality, and of sacrifice". Paraphrased on Days 3 and 8.
- **CCC 1261:** hope of salvation for children who die without Baptism (paraphrased, Day 7: "gives us reason to hope").
- **Dignitas Personae 12–13 (CDF, 2008):** treatments that remove obstacles to natural fertility (hormonal treatment, surgery for endometriosis, repair of the tubes) are licit, authentic treatments; adoption "should be encouraged, promoted and facilitated". Basis for "some doctors work in exactly this way" (Day 6) and "which the Church warmly encourages" (Day 8).
- **Donum Vitae II.B.5 (CDF, 1987):** "every child which comes into the world must in any case be accepted as a living gift of the divine Goodness and must be brought up with love." Basis for Day 6's last line, "every child, however conceived, is a gift from God", so parents of IVF children are not shamed.
- **Donum Vitae II.B.8:** the suffering of infertile spouses is one everyone must understand; the child is not an object to which one has a right; sterility can be the occasion for other important services to life (adoption, education, help to other families).

## Saint facts

- **Joachim and Anne:** not named in Scripture. Their names and the story of their childlessness come from the 2nd-century *Protoevangelium of James* (apocryphal, but the source of the Church's feasts of Mary's Nativity and Presentation): Joachim's offering refused at the Temple because he had no offspring, his 40 days of fasting in the wilderness, Anne's lament at home, an angel announcing the child to each, their meeting at the gate (Giotto, Scrovegni Chapel). Always introduced as "an ancient tradition". Feast: July 26 (memorial of Sts. Joachim and Anne, Parents of the Blessed Virgin Mary). Anne is the Greek/Latin form of the Hebrew Hannah.
- **Zechariah:** Luke 1:5–25, 57–79. Priest of the division of Abijah; he and Elizabeth "righteous before God"; struck silent for doubting Gabriel until John's naming (Luke 1:64); the Benedictus is prayed at Morning Prayer in the Liturgy of the Hours. Commemorated with St. Elizabeth on Nov 5 in the Roman Martyrology.
- **Our Lady of Hope:** the title of the 1871 apparition at Pontmain, France (approved 1872). Not explained in the text; used as a litany title for a novena about waiting.
