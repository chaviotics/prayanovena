# Novena for When God Seems Silent: sources

## Scripture (RSV-CE)

All texts are RSV-CE, checked word for word on 2026-11-24 by script against the RSVCE chapter files of the GitHub repository `andrewheekin/obsidian-holy-bible` (commit 9595b0c, chapter files headed "(RSVCE)"). Small-caps LORD restored where the source shows "theLord". The only differences are the source's footnote letter in Job 23:9 ("Ioturn") and two paragraph-opening quotation marks of continuing speech (Job 23:8, Hosea 2:14), left off as on the other novenas (see finishing-a-thesis, Matthew 11:28 and 6:34).

**No thee/thou.** Checked by script: none of the passages has thee, thou, thy, thine, hast, didst, doth, art, wilt or shalt. Passed over for that reason: Psalm 42:1 ("so longs my soul for thee"), Psalm 22:1 and Mark 15:34 ("why hast thou forsaken me"), Psalm 13:1, Psalm 63:1, Isaiah 45:15, Psalm 42:5 (the refrain is cut by the verse division and v.6 has "thee"; Psalm 42:11 has the whole refrain).

**Quotation marks.** Isaiah 49:15 was the first choice for Day 5, but its quotation stays open past the verse (also dropped by the expectant-parents novena); Day 5 uses Romans 8:38–39. Mark 4:26–27 (the seed growing secretly) opens a quotation that closes only at 4:29; Day 8 uses John 12:24 (inside Jesus' speech, no marks). John 6:67–69 opens and closes both speeches. Psalm 46:10 and John 20:29 are complete quotations.

**Overlap.** The parallel "Weary and Wounded Servants" novena uses Matthew 11:28–29, 1 Kings 19:5–7, Habakkuk 3:17–18, Psalm 55:12–14, Psalm 147:3–4, Numbers 11:14, John 21:15–16, Isaiah 43:19, Psalm 23:1–3, Psalm 3:5 and 2 Corinthians 4:16; none is used here.

## Saints

- **St. Thérèse of Lisieux** (1873–1897, feast Oct 1, Doctor of the Church 1997). *Story of a Soul*, Manuscript C, ch. IX "The Night of the Soul" (Thomas Taylor translation, clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/EN/gny.htm): the trial began "during the Paschal days" of 1896 (her first haemoptysis Good Friday, April 3, 1896) and lasted until her death (Sept 30, 1897); "it is not a veil—it is a wall which rises to the very heavens"; "I sing only of what I wish to believe"; "I have made more acts of Faith in this last year than during all the rest of my life." All used as paraphrase. Her well-known falling-asleep-at-prayer story is NOT used (Serving in Ministry, Day 6).
- **St. Jane Frances de Chantal** (Dijon 28 Jan 1572 – Moulins 13 Dec 1641; canonized 1767; feast Aug 12 in the current General Roman Calendar, formerly Aug 21/Dec 12). Catholic Encyclopedia "St. Jane Frances de Chantal" (newadvent.org/cathen/08282c.htm): husband killed "while out shooting" in 1601; met Francis de Sales in Lent 1604 at Dijon; Visitation established at Annecy 6 June 1610; long "interior crosses". Her own count of 41 years of temptations ("I've had these temptations for forty-one years now") and the 1614 letter line "Notwithstanding my absolute misery I hope in Him": visitationspirit.org/2013/01/year-of-faith-st-jane-de-chantal (from her Selected Letters). St. Vincent de Paul's testimony that she was full of faith yet tormented all her life by thoughts against it: marian.org/node/100005958 (paraphrased). Francis de Sales on "bare, dry acts performed solely by the superior will" as the highest point of religion, in a letter to her: aleteia.org/2022/01/29/st-francis-de-sales-advice-for-coping-with-spiritual-dryness (paraphrased).
- **St. Francis de Sales** (Thorens 21 Aug 1567 – Lyon 28 Dec 1622; Bishop of Geneva; Doctor of the Church 1877; feast Jan 24). The Paris crisis (c. 1586–87, a month or two by different accounts, so "for weeks"): fear that he was predestined to damnation; his prayer that he might at least love God in this life; the Memorare before the statue of Our Lady of Good Deliverance at Saint-Étienne-des-Grès, and the temptation ceasing: Catholic Encyclopedia (newadvent.org/cathen/06220a.htm), en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorare (quoting Camus), ncregister.com/blog/on-never-expecting-too-much-from-god. *Introduction to the Devout Life* II.9 "Concerning Dryness in Meditation" (ccel.org/ccel/desales/devout_life/devout_life.iv.ix.html): "do not be disturbed at your dryness, however great it be"; courtiers who "appear a hundred times at court without any hope of a word from their king, but merely to pay their homage and be seen of him"; "remain calmly and devoutly before Him" (paraphrased, Day 3).
- **St. John of the Cross** (cited for teaching only, Day 8; he is an intercessor in the Weary Servants novena): *The Dark Night*, Book I, God withdrawing sensible consolation so the soul loves Him for Himself (paraphrased).
- **Our Lady of Solitude** (Nuestra Señora de la Soledad): Mary in her solitude on Holy Saturday; a traditional title much loved in Spain, Mexico and the Philippines (Soledad de Porta Vaga, Cavite).

## Catechism

CCC 2731 (dryness: "the moment of sheer faith clinging faithfully to Jesus in his agony and in his tomb"), CCC 1374 (the whole Christ truly, really and substantially present), CCC 1128 (sacraments act by the power of Christ's saving work, ex opere operato).
