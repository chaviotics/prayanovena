## Range checks

- Matthew 5:4 was the first choice for Day 2 and was dropped: in the RSVCE it opens with a quotation mark (the Sermon on the Mount) that does not close within the verse. John 11:35 replaced it.
- Quoted outside a day's verse: "Jesus wept" (Day 2, its own verse). Other Scripture is paraphrased and cited by reference only: Luke 2:35, John 2:1–5, Matthew 1:19 (RSVCE: Joseph "resolved to send her away quietly"; paraphrased as "part from her quietly").
- Permission limits: 17 verses in total; no whole book, far under 25% of any book.

## Saint facts (checked 2026-11-22)

- St. Pier Giorgio Frassati: born Turin 6 April 1901, died there 4 July 1925 of polio, aged 24; joined a St. Vincent de Paul conference in 1918; drawn to a young woman as a student but never pursued her because he was unsure his parents would approve, and confided this to his sister; thousands lined the streets at his funeral, to the surprise of family and friends; motto "Verso l'alto"; beatified 20 May 1990 (John Paul II), canonized 7 September 2025 (Leo XIV) with Carlo Acutis; feast 4 July. Popular patronages include young people, students and mountaineers (none official). Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Giorgio_Frassati; FrassatiUSA, "His love for Laura" (frassatiusa-org.sites.ecatholic.com/his-love-for-laura, citing the canonization documents and his letters to Isidoro Bonini) for: he never told her, his parents' strained marriage, and that in giving her up he wished her happiness (paraphrased, not quoted). Left out on purpose because not confirmed in this check: the young woman's name (often given as Laura Hidalgo) and the tram-fare story.
- St. Augustine of Hippo (added 2026-11-22 at Chav's choice): 354–430, feast 28 August, Bishop of Hippo, Doctor of the Church, son of St. Monica. *Confessions* 6.15: the woman he had lived with for years (mother of his son Adeodatus) was sent back to Africa as an impediment to the marriage arranged for him; his heart, which clung to her, was wounded and bleeding (paraphrased). *Confessions* 1.1: "You have made us for Yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in You" (paraphrased, not quoted).
- St. Josephine Bakhita (Day 6 story, not an intercessor): kidnapped as a child in Sudan and sold into slavery; in Italy she came to know God, was baptized (1890) and became a Canossian sister; died 1947, canonized 2000. Benedict XVI, *Spe Salvi* 3, tells how knowing she was loved by God gave her hope and made her free (paraphrased).
- St. Joseph: Matthew 1:18–25; no words of his are recorded in the Gospels.
- CCC 1604: "God who created man out of love also calls him to love — the fundamental and innate vocation of every human being."

## Other notes

- The closing prayer's "Your Sacred Heart knows what it is to love and not be loved in return" echoes the devotion to the Sacred Heart (St. Margaret Mary Alacoque) and John 1:11; it is not a quotation.
- Day 5's Cana paragraph keeps to what John 2:1–5 says (changed after review).
