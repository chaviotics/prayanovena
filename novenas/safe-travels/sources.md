# Novena for Safe Travels: sources

## Scripture (RSV-CE)

All texts are RSV-CE, checked word for word on 2026-11-25 against the RSVCE chapter files of the GitHub repository `andrewheekin/obsidian-holy-bible` (raw.githubusercontent.com; chapter files headed "(RSVCE)"). Small-caps LORD restored where the source shows "theLord". The source's pronunciation marks in names (Raphʹael, Tobiʹas, Gabʹael) are left off, as on the other novenas. Poetry lines (Psalm 121) are split with " / " as printed in the RSV.

**No thee/thou.** None of the passages has thee, thou, thy, thine, hast, didst, doth, art, wilt or shalt. Passed over for that reason: Psalm 139:9–10 ("thy hand shall lead me").

**Quotation marks.** Genesis 12:1 (Abram's call) opens a quotation that closes only at 12:3, so Day 2 uses Hebrews 11:8. Tobit 5:4 ends mid-sentence ("who was an angel,"), so Day 3 runs 5:4–6, where both speeches open and close. Matthew 2:13–14 ends mid-sentence ("departed to Egypt,"), so Joseph's flight is quoted in a reflection only; Day 8 uses Matthew 2:19–21, complete. Numbers 6:24–26 and Joshua 1:9 end inside open quotations and were not used. Jeremiah 29:7 sits inside the letter of 29:4–23 but carries no quotation marks itself (as John 12:24 on the spiritual-dryness novena). Mark 4:39–40 and Luke 24:15–16 are complete.

## Saints

- **St. Raphael the Archangel** (feast Sep 29 with Sts. Michael and Gabriel, since the 1969 calendar). Book of Tobit 5–12: he goes with Tobias to Media unrecognized, as "Azarias" (5:12), and reveals himself at the end (12:15). Patron of travelers by long tradition (from Tobit). Portrait reused from the healing novena.
- **St. Joseph** (feasts Mar 19 and May 1). Matthew 2:13–23: the flight into Egypt by night, the return after Herod's death, the fear of Archelaus and the move to Nazareth. Portrait reused from the debt novena.
- **St. Frances Xavier Cabrini** (Sant'Angelo Lodigiano 15 July 1850 – Chicago 22 Dec 1917). Founded the Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus (1880); sent by Pope Leo XIII to the Italian immigrants of the United States ("not to the East, but to the West"), arriving in New York in 1889; founded schools, orphanages and hospitals in the Americas and Europe; naturalized US citizen (1909); canonized 7 July 1946, the first US citizen saint; named patron of immigrants by Pius XII (1950). Feast Nov 13 in the US calendar; Dec 22 (her death) in the Roman Martyrology. Her lifelong fear of water and her many ocean crossings are told in the standard biographies (e.g. Catholic Company, "Which saint was deathly afraid of water?"; Franciscan Media, Saint of the Day). The number of crossings varies between sources, so the text says only "again and again".
- **Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage** (Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje), the image venerated at Antipolo, Rizal, Philippines, brought from Mexico on the Manila galleon in 1626 and long invoked by travelers. Used as the Marian title only; no history is told in the novena. The litany picture is Mary from a Murillo painting of the Holy Family's rest on the flight into Egypt, not the Antipolo image (its photographs are not public domain).
