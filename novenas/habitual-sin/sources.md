# Novena for Freedom from Habitual Sin: sources

## Scripture (RSV-CE)

All texts are RSV-CE, checked word for word on 2026-11-24 against the RSVCE chapter files in the GitHub repository `andrewheekin/obsidian-holy-bible` (headed "(RSVCE)", fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com), compared by script (only non-breaking spaces and the source's footnote letter in Psalm 51:10, "rightbspirit", differ).

**No thee/thou.** None of the passages has archaic address. Lamentations 3:22–23 ("great is thy faithfulness") was passed over for that reason.

**Quotation marks.** John 8:34–36 was the first choice for Day 3, but Jesus' speech runs on past verse 36, so the range would end inside an open quotation; Day 3 uses John 8:36 alone (inside the speech, so no quotation marks), and the reflection paraphrases 8:34. Romans 13:12–14 and 13:13–14 both begin mid-sentence in lowercase, so Day 5 uses Romans 13:14 and the app-only paragraph quotes 13:13. Psalm 51:10 and Micah 7:8 keep the RSV's "O God", "O my enemy" (vocative, not archaic). Micah 7:8 is set as poetry.

## St. Augustine, *Confessions*

- 8.7.17: as a youth he had prayed "Give me chastity and continence, only not yet" (Pusey); Day 1 uses the common rendering "Grant me chastity and continence, but not yet" (Chadwick-style wording).
- 8.11.25: "Let it be now, let it be now" (basis for "until the 'not yet' became 'now'", Day 1).
- 8.5.10: the enemy made a chain of his will; from a perverse will came lust, lust served became habit, habit not resisted became necessity (paraphrased, Day 3).
- 8.12.28–29: the garden in Milan, weeping, a child's voice "Take up and read" (*tolle lege*), Romans 13:13–14, "a light of certainty" flooding his heart (Day 5).
- 9.8.18: Monica as a girl sent to draw wine, sipping secretly, adding little by little until she drank nearly full cups; a maidservant quarreling with her called her a drunkard (*meribibula*); she saw her fault and gave it up at once (Day 4).
- 3.12.21: a bishop asked by Monica to reason with her son declined, then said "it is not possible that the son of these tears should perish" (Day 8).
- 9.1.1: "You have broken my bonds" (Psalm 116:16, as Augustine's thanksgiving) (Day 9).

## St. Charles de Foucauld (replaced St. Camillus de Lellis on 2026-11-24, Chav's choice)

Born 1858 in Strasbourg, orphaned young, lost his faith as a teenager; cavalry officer (Saint-Cyr, Saumur) known for lavish spending of his inheritance on parties and fine food; placed on inactive status (suspended) in 1881 for indiscipline after refusing to give up the mistress he had brought to Algeria; explored Morocco 1883–84; in late October 1886 went to Abbé Henri Huvelin at the church of Saint-Augustin in Paris to ask for instruction, was told to kneel and confess, and did (his conversion). Trappist 1890, Nazareth, priest 1901; lived at Beni Abbès and then Tamanrasset among the Tuareg, long hours of adoration, wished to be a "universal brother"; killed 1 December 1916. Beatified 2005, canonized 15 May 2022; memorial Dec 1. "Hermit of the Sahara" is a description, not a formal patronage. Details left out as not certain enough: the "My God, if You exist" prayer wording, the mistress's name.

## Church teaching (Catechism)

- CCC 1865: sin creates a proclivity to sin; it engenders vice by repetition (Day 3).
- CCC 1735: imputability can be diminished by habit, among other factors (Day 6).
- CCC 2342: "Self-mastery is a long and exacting work" (Day 6, quoted).
- CCC 1810: human virtues acquired by repeated efforts are purified and elevated by grace (Day 7).
- Litany of Loreto: "Refuge of sinners" (Day 6, litany).
- Act of Contrition: "to avoid the near occasion of sin" (Day 5).
