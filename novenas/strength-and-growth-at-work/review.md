# Review: Novena for Work and Livelihood

Doctrine is sound throughout: work as a share in God's care before the Fall, dignity from the image of God rather than a job title, Sunday rest, just wages, honest trade, and a Day 9 that is gentle with those still waiting ("never a sign that God has abandoned us or that our faith was too weak"). No guarantees, no prosperity framing, no chain elements. The CCC citations all say what the text claims: 2184–2185 (Sunday rest), 2427–2428 (work honors the Creator's gifts; work is for man), 2434 (just wage; withholding it can be a grave injustice), 2409 (business fraud, unjust wages). Every word count is within the skill table, the digit rules hold, the "Praying with others" tip matches the other novenas word for word, and the Day 9 step is exact. Titles are correct ("Our Mother of Perpetual Help" is the usual form, e.g. at Baclaran). `alt` and `credits` were not reviewed (images are being chosen separately).

Confirmed:
- Joseph: May 1 feast instituted by Pius XII in 1955; Matthew 13:55 "the carpenter's son".
- Cajetan: born in Vicenza 1480, died in Naples 7 August 1547; Theatines erected 1524; feast 7 August; the Theatines' strict poverty and reliance on Providence; patron of the unemployed; San Cayetano in Buenos Aires, "pan y trabajo".
- Homobonus: married cloth merchant of Cremona; he and his wife lived simply; died 13 November 1197; canonized by Innocent III on 12 January 1199, about 14 months later; "father of the poor" in the bull; patronages; feast 13 November.

1. **MUST: saints[2].bio, sentence 2 (Homobonus "among the first").** "Canonized in 1199, among the first lay people raised to the altars who were neither martyrs nor royalty." This is not certain enough to keep:
   - sources.md says it was not found in the sources checked.
   - The scholarly claim (Vauchez; Prudlo, *Merchant Saint: The Church, the Market, and the First Lay Canonization*) is framed differently, roughly as the first canonized layman who was not of royal or noble birth. "Neither martyrs nor royalty" says something else, and it leaves out nobility, which is the point.
   - "Raised to the altars" also covers pre-papal, local cults, where "among the first" is hard to defend.
   
   Prefer cutting over hedging. Replace with something certain and just as striking (28 words): "Married cloth merchant of Cremona, known for honest trade and generosity to the poor. Canonized by Pope Innocent III in 1199, just over a year after his death."

2. **MUST: days[7].more, sentences 1–2, and days[7].prayer (Cajetan and the monte di pietà).** "In Naples he helped found a monte di pietà, a charitable fund that lent money on pawned goods to protect the poor from usurers." The Naples Monte di Pietà (1539, later the Banco di Napoli) is credited by its own history to Neapolitan laymen, Aurelio Paparo and Leonardo di Palma. Cajetan's part rests on hagiography (Franciscan Media; some encyclopedia summaries), and historians do not agree on it. It is not a fact to stake the novena on. The prayer's "who protected the poor from usurers" depends on the same claim.
   - days[7].more: replace "St. Cajetan cared for the poor in practical ways. In Naples he helped found a monte di pietà, a charitable fund that lent money on pawned goods to protect the poor from usurers." with "St. Cajetan cared for the poor and the sick in practical ways. In his home city of Vicenza he founded a hospital for the incurably ill, and he served the sick with his own hands." (The hospital for incurables at Vicenza is in the Catholic Encyclopedia article already cited in sources.md. Paragraph becomes 75 words, at the limit. If you want room, drop "with his own hands" and end "…and he served the sick himself.")
   - days[7].prayer: replace "and of St. Cajetan, who protected the poor from usurers," with "and of St. Cajetan, who served the sick and the poor,". (78 words)
   - sources.md: remove the monte di pietà line and note the change.

3. **SHOULD: tips[1].p ("(Day 3)" is attached to the wrong item).** "…update your CV, ask someone you trust for advice, or talk honestly with your staff (Day&nbsp;3)." Day 3's step is the CV/application/ask-someone step; talking honestly with staff belongs to Day 6. As written, the pointer seems to send business owners to Day 3 for the staff conversation. Fix: "Pair prayer with one prudent step (Day&nbsp;3): update your CV, ask someone you trust for advice, or talk honestly with your staff."

4. **SHOULD: days[2].refl ("long invoked").** "St. Cajetan, long invoked by people looking for work, reminds us that we never knock alone." His patronage of the unemployed is real but largely modern; the mass Argentine devotion dates from the 20th century. In Italy his older title is "saint of Providence" (used on Day 2). "Long" is not needed. Fix: "St. Cajetan, invoked by so many people looking for work, reminds us that we never knock alone." (81 words)

5. **SHOULD (minor): days[1].refl (Theatine poverty, precision).** "His Theatine priests took a strict vow of poverty and owned nothing, trusting God to provide." The distinctive point (Catholic Encyclopedia, Theatines) is that they owned no property and did not even beg, but lived on what was freely offered. Optional sharper wording: "His Theatine priests owned nothing and did not even beg, trusting God to provide." (78 words)

6. **SHOULD (minor): closingVerse length.** Psalm 90:17 is 30 words, over the table's "about 25" (the other novenas run 17–28). It is one verse ending at a full stop, so it is acceptable. Note it only if the print closing gets crowded; Psalm 90:17a alone ends at a comma, so do not trim it.
