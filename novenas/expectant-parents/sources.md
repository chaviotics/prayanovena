# Novena for Expectant Parents: Scripture sources

All texts are NABRE (New American Bible, revised edition), checked word for word on 2026-11-21 against bible.usccb.org.

**How they were checked.** Each chapter page was read with WebFetch (one fetch per chapter). The fetch tool will not quote more than about 125 characters at once, so the longer verses were checked by comparing the exact text below against the page character by character: Matthew 1:20–21 (whole passage) and the last sentence of Luke 1:38. Both came back as an exact match. Footnote letters and asterisks are left out. The divine name is printed "LORD" as on the page; the site shows it in small caps.

**Line breaks.** " / " marks a poetry line break as printed on bible.usccb.org (Psalm 139:13, Numbers 6:24–26, Psalm 56:4, Jeremiah 1:5, Psalm 34:19). The USCCB terms ask for poetic line breaks to be kept.

## Numbering changes (RSV-CE → NABRE)

- Psalm 56:3 → **Psalm 56:4** (the NABRE counts the psalm's title as verse 1; confirmed on the chapter page).
- Psalm 34:18 → **Psalm 34:19** (same reason; confirmed).
- Psalm 139:13 stays 139:13: the title "For the leader. A psalm of David." is printed inside verse 1, so the numbering does not shift.
- All other references are unchanged.

## Range checks

- No passage starts or ends in the middle of a quotation, and each ends at the end of a sentence.
  - Psalm 56:4 begins in mid-sentence in the NABRE: verse 3 ends "O Most High," and verse 4 continues "when I am afraid, / in you I place my trust." It is quoted as printed, with its lowercase "when"; the verse ends with a full stop. (Starting at "O Most High," would need a part of verse 3.)
  - Psalm 34:19's second line has no subject of its own ("saves those whose spirit is crushed."); quoted as printed.
  - Jeremiah 1:5 is printed as poetry without quotation marks in the NABRE (verse 4: "The word of the LORD came to me:"), so no quote mark dangles.
  - Numbers 6:24–26 is printed without quotation marks after "Say to them:" (6:23); each line ends with "!".
  - Matthew 1:20–21 opens and closes the angel's words. Luke 1:38 closes Mary's words and ends with a narrative sentence.
  - John 16:21 sits inside Jesus' discourse; the verse itself carries no quotation marks.
  - First choices dropped earlier for the same reason: Luke 1:30 (the angel's words run on to v.33), Isaiah 49:15 (the quotation stays open), Luke 1:41–42 (Elizabeth's words run to v.45).
- The epigraph has 12 words; the closing verse has 30, against "up to about 25": kept, because the priestly blessing is the natural blessing over a child and parents.
- Text quoted in reflections, prayers and steps comes only from the day's own verse: "May it be done" (Luke 1:38, Day 9 prayer). Other Scripture is paraphrased without quote marks and cited by reference only: Luke 1:24, 1:29–30, 1:41, 1:56, 1:58; Luke 2:4–7; Matthew 2:13–14; Exodus 1:15 (the midwives' names).
- Echoes of the old RSV wording were brought into line with the NABRE: Day 1 "places his trust"; Day 4 "do not be afraid" (was "do not fear"); Day 5 "His peace guards our hearts and minds"; Day 7 "the hour arrives", "a child has been born into the world", "anguish gives way to joy"; Day 8 "the Lord is close", "You are close to the brokenhearted"; Day 9 prayer "May it be done" (was "Let it be"). Paraphrases now follow the NABRE's words: Day 1 "went into seclusion" (Luke 1:24), Day 3 "her relative Elizabeth" (Luke 1:36), Day 4 "no room for them in the inn" (Luke 2:7), Day 9 "neighbors and relatives … his great mercy toward her" (Luke 1:58).

## Saint facts (checked 2026-11-21)

- St. Gerard Majella: born 6 April 1726, Muro Lucano; father a tailor; refused by the Capuchins for poor health; Redemptorist lay brother from 1749; falsely accused, kept silent, later cleared when the accuser retracted; died 16 October 1755 at Materdomini, aged 29, of tuberculosis; beatified 1893, canonized 1904; feast 16 October. Handkerchief story as told by EWTN ("Hope in the Handkerchief of a Saint") and Wikipedia; told here as "a story told since his time". Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Majella; ewtn.com/catholicism/library/hope-in-the-handkerchief-of-a-saint-5537.
- St. Elizabeth: everything from Luke 1 (5–7, 24, 36, 39–45, 56–58).
- St. Gianna Beretta Molla (Day 6, not an intercessor): Vatican biography (vatican.va, canonization 16 May 2004): clinic in Mesero 1950, pediatrics at Milan 1952, married Pietro Molla 24 September 1955, four children.

## Church teaching and rites referred to

- CCC 2270 (life protected from conception), Day 2. CCC 1261 (children who die without Baptism: "allow us to hope"), Day 8. CCC 1256 (anyone may baptize in an emergency), tip "If the road is hard".
- *Book of Blessings*: Order for the Blessing of Parents before Childbirth (nos. 215–252; a priest or deacon may give it). USCCB Rite for the Blessing of a Child in the Womb (Holy See confirmation 2012). USCCB Blessing of Parents after a Miscarriage or Stillbirth. Funeral rites for children who die before Baptism (canon 1183 §2).

## Review

An independent Catholic review (review.md) found 1 error, 5 improvements and 6 minor points; all applied on 2026-11-21 except the note on St. Joseph's share of the days (no change needed):
- Day 1: the angel's first words to Mary were the greeting (Luke 1:28), not "Do not be afraid"; the paragraph now paraphrases and no longer says Elizabeth hid out of fear.
- Day 8: CCC 1261 "allows us to hope"; names the funeral rites and the blessing for grieving parents; no implied happy ending.
- Day 7: the handkerchief story ends "We ask his prayers in trust, not as a charm."
- Day 4: prayer and step now include a mother waiting without the child's father.
- Tips: the Church's blessing of parents before childbirth or of a child in the womb (priest or deacon); "me" or "us"; emergency Baptism and the Church's care after a loss.
- Opening prayer adds "whatever lies ahead"; card says "the baby's health" instead of "a healthy child"; kicker "9 days of hope for parents and their baby"; Elizabeth bio and "foster father" wording.
