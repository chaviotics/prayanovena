# Novena for the Healing of the Sick: Scripture sources

All texts are NABRE (New American Bible, revised edition), checked word for word on 2026-11-21 against bible.usccb.org.

**How they were checked.** Each chapter page (`https://bible.usccb.org/bible/<book>/<chapter>`) was fetched and the verses read off it. The Galatians page at `/bible/galatians/6` returned only headings and footnotes, so the text was read from `/bible/gal/6`, the same chapter under the short book name. The page text prints the divine name as "LORD", and that is kept. Poetry line breaks follow the page and appear as " / " (the build renders them as line breaks). Sirach 38:13–14 and Psalm 147:3 begin some lines with a capital after a comma ("For", "That", "Healing"). The NABRE prints them that way, a second check confirmed it, and the capitals are kept.

## Numbering

- Psalm 23: the NABRE title "A psalm of David." is part of verse 1, so the verse keeps its RSV number (23:4).
- Psalm 147: no numbered title line, so the numbers match the RSV.
- Sirach 38 and 3 John: same numbering as the RSV. 3 John has 15 verses on the USCCB page.

## Range changes (old RSV ref → NABRE ref)

- **Day 5: Sirach 38:13–14 → Sirach 38:12–14.** In the NABRE, verse 13 ("For there are times…") finishes the sentence that verse 12 begins ("Then give the doctor his place / lest he leave; you need him too,"). Adding verse 12 lets the passage start at the beginning of a sentence, and it suits the day's theme of honoring doctors.
- **Day 7: Matthew 8:17 → Matthew 8:16–17.** NABRE verse 17 begins in the middle of a sentence ("to fulfill what had been said…"), so verse 16 is added to start at the beginning of the sentence.
- **Day 8: Psalm 147:3 → Psalm 147:2–3.** NABRE verse 3 is a participle clause ("Healing the brokenhearted, / and binding up their wounds.") that continues verse 2's sentence, so verse 2 is added.

## Range checks

- No passage starts or ends in the middle of a quotation, and each one ends at the end of a sentence.
  - John 11:3, Mark 1:40–41 and Matthew 8:16–17 open and close their quotations within the passage.
  - Sirach 38:12–14 and Psalm 147:2–3 now start at the beginning of their sentences (see above).
- Epigraph: 19 words. Closing verse: 20 words. Both are within the "about 25 words" limit, so the closing verse no longer goes over it as the RSV verse did.
- Text quoted in day reflections, prayers and steps is NABRE, taken from the day passages:
  - "If you wish, you can" and "if you wish" (Mark 1:40). These replace the RSV's "If you will, you can".
  - "for you are with me" (Psalm 23:4). This replaces the RSV's "for thou art with me".
- Prayers and reflections that lean on a verse were brought into line with the NABRE wording:
  - Day 5 reflection: "they, too, need prayer" became "they, too, pray to God", and it now says to give doctors their place (Sirach 38:12, 14).
  - Day 7 prayer: "You took away our infirmities and bore our diseases" (Matthew 8:17).
  - Day 9 reflection: "all things work for good for those who love God" (Romans 8:28).
- Other Scripture is paraphrased without quote marks and cited by reference only: John 11:35, Mark 2:5, Tobit 11, Tobit 12, Tobit 12:6, 2 Corinthians 12:8–9.
- **St. Raphael's bio** (Tobit 12:15) used to echo the RSV ("one of the seven holy angels who present the prayers of the saints"). It now paraphrases the NABRE without quote marks: "one of the seven angels who stand and serve before the Lord". NABRE Tobit 12:15, checked at https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/12, reads: "I am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand and serve before the Glory of the Lord.” Tobit 12:6 (Raphael urging them to bless and thank God) was checked on the same page.

## Permission

The NABRE may be quoted up to 5,000 words site-wide without written permission, as long as no complete book is quoted and the quotations make up less than 40% of the work. This novena quotes 17 verses: the epigraph, the closing verse and the nine day passages, about 305 words in all, plus a few short phrases repeated from them in the reflections. No complete book is quoted. 3 John has 15 verses, and one of them is used.

## Review

An independent Catholic review (review.md) found 3 errors and 8 improvements, and all were applied on 2026-11-20:
- St. Camillus's leg wound: "for the rest of his life", not "all his life".
- At Lourdes, Our Lady told Bernadette to drink and wash at the spring (she didn't tell her to dig).
- Jesus wept before He reached the tomb (John 11:35, 11:38).
- The Anointing of the Sick is explained (CCC 1514–1515).
- Healing that doesn't come is never a sign of weak faith (CCC 1508).
- Added the hope of eternal life (CCC 1505).
- Added a warning against false "sure cures" (CCC 2117).
- Wording fixes on Days 2, 4, 5, 7 and 9.
