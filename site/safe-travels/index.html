      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, every road we take is known to You. We bring You a journey: a trip for work or rest, a long flight or drive, or a season of work far from home for those we love. Give us peace in place of worry, and watch over those who wait. Today we ask Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage, long loved by travelers; St. Raphael, who walked beside young Tobias; St. Joseph, who led the Holy Family into Egypt and home again; and St. Frances Xavier Cabrini, who crossed the sea to care for those far from home, to join their prayers with ours, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        Lifting Our Eyes

        Before any journey, worries come easily: the flight, the roads, the weather, the work waiting at the other end, the people we leave behind. The psalmist lifts his eyes to the hills and asks the honest question, “From whence does my help come?” Then he answers it: from the LORD, who made heaven and earth. The One who made every mountain, sea and city we will pass is not a stranger to any of them. So we begin these nine days by naming our worries and lifting our eyes to Him.

        Psalm 121 is one of the “Songs of Ascents” (Psalms 120–134), sung by pilgrims as they traveled up to Jerusalem for the great feasts. They walked rough roads through the hills, often for days, and they prayed as they went. Their song is a traveler’s prayer, and it can be ours on a highway, in an airport, on a ship at sea, or at a bus stop before dawn.

      3. 3Pray

        Heavenly Father, we bring You this journey and every worry that comes with it. We lift our eyes to You, who made heaven and earth. Our help comes from You, not from our planning alone. Calm our anxious thoughts, and keep our hearts close to You from the first step to the last. Watch over all who travel today, and those who wait for them. Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage, pray for us.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage, pray for us.

        St. Raphael, pray for us.

        St. Joseph, pray for us.

        St. Frances Xavier Cabrini, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, You walked the roads of Galilee and drew near to two travelers on the way to Emmaus. Walk with us on every road, stay with those we love, and bring us at last to our home with You. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

        Today’s step

        Write down the journey you are praying for: who is going, where and when. Keep it with you through these nine days.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention. If the day has a picture, take a moment to look at it; it is there to help you see the passage.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Mass before a long trip, and to Confession; many parishes will gladly bless travelers. Pray together with those you leave behind, and agree on a time to call. Then do your part: rest before you drive, keep to safety rules, and share your plans with someone at home. A medal or prayer card is a reminder to pray, not a charm: we trust God, not objects.

      Working abroad

      Before you sign, check that the job and the agency are licensed (for Filipinos, with the Department of Migrant Workers, DMW), and never pay an unlicensed recruiter. Never let anyone keep your passport, and keep a copy of your contract. If you are ever in trouble, contact your country’s nearest embassy or consulate.

      The 3 Intercessors

      St. Raphael

      Archangel · Feast · Sep 29 (with Sts. Michael & Gabriel)

      Patron of travelers

      Archangel of the Book of Tobit, one of the seven holy angels. Unrecognized, he walked beside young Tobias on a long journey, guided him, and brought him home again.

      St. Joseph

      1st century · Feasts · Mar 19 & May 1

      Patron of the Church

      Warned in a dream, he rose by night and took Mary and the Child to Egypt, living as a foreigner until God called him home to Israel and Nazareth.

      St. Frances Xavier Cabrini

      1850–1917 · Feast · Nov 13 (US) · Dec 22

      Patron of immigrants

      An Italian nun sent by Pope Leo XIII to the immigrants of New York. Though afraid of water, she crossed the ocean again and again to found schools, orphanages and hospitals.
