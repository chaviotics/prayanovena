      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross. Where you see N., say the name of the person you are praying for.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, You know the exam that N. is preparing for: the long hours of study, the pressure and the hopes that rest on it, and the worry of all who love them. We place this exam in Your hands, and we trust in Your care more than in our fear. Give N. diligence, a clear mind and an honest heart, and let Your will be done. Today we ask Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom; St. Joseph of Cupertino, friend of struggling students; and St. Thomas Aquinas, who prayed before he studied, to join their prayers with ours before Your throne, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        Honest About Our Worry

        Before an important exam, worry can sit like a weight on the chest: review notes that never seem to end, the cost of school and review fees, the fear of letting down the people who sacrificed for us. God does not scold us for that fear. He takes our right hand, the very hand that will hold the pen, and says, “Fear not, I will help you.” We begin these nine days by telling Him honestly how we feel.

        St. Joseph of Cupertino knew what it was to feel slow and behind. As a young man in Copertino, in southern Italy, he struggled with his studies and found learning hard. Yet God drew him close. He became a Franciscan friar and a priest, so absorbed in prayer that he was known for his ecstasies. He is a friend to every student who fears they are not clever enough.

      3. 3Pray

        Heavenly Father, You know N. by name, and You know how heavy this exam feels for them and for all of us who love them. We are anxious, and we tell You so. Take N. by the hand, as You promised, and quiet the fear that makes it hard to think. Through the prayers of St. Joseph of Cupertino, give N. the courage to keep going, one day and one page at a time.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom, pray for us.

        St. Joseph of Cupertino, pray for us.

        St. Thomas Aquinas, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, Wisdom of the Father, bless N. as they prepare and on the day of the exam. Give them a clear mind, an honest heart and peace in Your holy will, whatever the result. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention. If the day has a picture, take a moment to look at it; it is there to help you see the passage.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days, and receive Communion before the exam. Pair prayer with good preparation: a study plan (Day 4), enough sleep (Day 5), and no leaked questions, copied answers or fixers (Day 6). If the result is not what you hoped, be gentle with yourself and others; many pass on a retake. If worry or disappointment becomes too heavy to carry, talk to someone you trust: a parent, a priest or a counselor. At “N.”, say the student’s name; if you are praying for yourself, say “me”.

      The 2 Intercessors

      St. Joseph of Cupertino

      1603–1663 · Feast · Sep 18

      Patron of students & those taking exams

      Franciscan friar from Copertino in southern Italy who struggled with learning, yet was ordained a priest and became known for his ecstasies in prayer. Canonized in 1767.

      St. Thomas Aquinas

      c. 1225–1274 · Feast · Jan 28

      Patron of students & Catholic schools

      Dominican friar and theologian, author of the Summa Theologiae and of hymns for Corpus Christi. A Doctor of the Church, named patron of Catholic schools by Pope Leo XIII in 1880.
