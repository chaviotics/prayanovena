      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross. Where you see N., say the name of the person you are praying for.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, Creator of heaven and earth, You have given N. a deep desire to know: to understand Your world, ask great questions and find the truth. Among so many voices, it is easy to grow restless or proud. Send Your Holy Spirit with His gifts of knowledge and wisdom, and draw N. to You, the source of truth. Today we ask Our Lady of Good Counsel; St. Augustine, restless seeker who found rest in You; St. Albert the Great, student of Your creation; and St. Hildegard of Bingen, mystic who praised You in Your works, to join their prayers with ours before Your throne, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        The Desire to Know

        God has put eternity into the human mind. That is why we keep asking questions: how the stars move, why the world holds together, what our life is for. The hunger to know is not a distraction from faith; it is God’s own gift, a thread that leads back to Him. Yet the same verse admits our limits: we cannot find out all that God has done. Today we bring Him the searching mind of the one we pray for, with its wonder and its restlessness.

        St. Augustine knew that restlessness well. As a brilliant young teacher of rhetoric, he tried ambition, pleasure, philosophy and a sect that claimed to explain everything, and none of it satisfied him. Years later he began his Confessions by telling God that He has made us for Himself, and our heart is restless until it rests in Him. Our searching is nothing to be ashamed of. It is the heart looking for its home.

      3. 3Pray

        Heavenly Father, You have put eternity into our minds, and You know the questions N. carries: about the universe, about life and about You. We thank You for their love of learning and their wonder at Your world. Through the prayers of St. Augustine, who searched so long before he found rest in You, and of St. Albert and St. Hildegard, keep N.’s searching honest and open, and let every question lead them closer to You.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Our Lady of Good Counsel, pray for us.

        St. Augustine of Hippo, pray for us.

        St. Albert the Great, pray for us.

        St. Hildegard of Bingen, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, Word through whom all things were made, send us the Spirit of truth You promised. Open our minds to understand, our eyes to see Your glory in creation, and our hearts to love You above all. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

        Today’s step

        Write down one question about God, life or the universe that you carry, and offer it to God at the start of this novena.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days. If you have not been baptized or confirmed, ask your parish about it; Confirmation increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us. Read Scripture slowly (Day 6), and learn from a good book or teacher. For deeper prayer, look for a trustworthy guide, such as a priest or spiritual director, and read the Catholic mystics (Day 7). Let learning lead to humility, never to pride (Day 8). At “N.”, say the person’s name; if you are praying for yourself, say “me”.

      The 3 Intercessors

      St. Augustine of Hippo

      354–430 · Feast · Aug 28

      Patron of theologians

      Son of St. Monica, he searched restlessly for truth through philosophy and false teachings before finding it in Christ. Baptized in 387, he became Bishop of Hippo and wrote the Confessions.

      St. Albert the Great

      c. 1200–1280 · Feast · Nov 15

      Patron of natural scientists

      Dominican friar, bishop and teacher of St. Thomas Aquinas, who wrote on plants, animals and minerals as well as theology. Declared a Doctor of the Church in 1931.

      St. Hildegard of Bingen

      1098–1179 · Feast · Sep 17

      Mystic & Doctor of the Church

      Benedictine abbess, mystic and visionary who wrote on nature and medicine and composed sacred music. Pope Benedict XVI declared her a Doctor of the Church in 2012.
