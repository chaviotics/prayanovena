      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross. Where you see N., say the name of the person you are praying for.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, You know N.’s thesis: the months of work, the deadlines, the drafts that come back marked, and the days when finishing feels impossible. We bring You this work and its weariness. Give N. perseverance, a clear mind, an honest heart and the humility to ask for help, and bring this work to completion according to Your will. We ask Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom; St. Thomas Aquinas, who sought the truth in every question; St. Albert the Great, teacher and patron of scientists; and St. Giuseppe Moscati, who served You in the laboratory and at the bedside, to join their prayers with ours, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        Bringing the Weight to God

        A thesis can become a weight we carry everywhere: in class, at meals, on the way to sleep. Some of us are behind schedule and ashamed to say it. Some have not opened the file in weeks. Jesus does not wait for us to have it all together before we come. He calls those who labor and are heavy laden, just as we are, and He offers rest. We begin these nine days by telling Him honestly where this work stands, and how we feel about it.

        Many theses take longer than planned. Experiments fail, advisers change, families need help, health gives way. Being behind is not the same as being a failure, and it is not a sign that God has forgotten us. Shame tells us to hide; Jesus tells us to come. Whatever the situation, the next step is the same: bring it into the light, first before God, and then before someone who can help.

      3. 3Pray

        Lord Jesus, You call all who labor and are heavy laden, and today we come to You with N. and this thesis. You know how far along it is, how long it has taken, and every worry and regret that comes with it. Lift the shame that makes N. want to hide, and give them Your rest. Through the prayers of Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom, help N. begin again today, right where they are.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Our Lady, Seat of Wisdom, pray for us.

        St. Thomas Aquinas, pray for us.

        St. Albert the Great, pray for us.

        St. Giuseppe Moscati, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, eternal Word of the Father and Truth itself, stay beside N. in this long work. Give them strength for today’s part of it, and peace in Your holy will, whatever comes. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

        Today’s step

        Write down honestly where the thesis stands today: what is done, what is left, and the next deadline. Keep it where you pray.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days, and receive Communion before your defense or submission. Pair prayer with practical steps: one small task a day (Day 2), regular updates to your adviser (Day 4), and honest sources, data and words (Day 6). Many schools offer writing help and counseling, so use them. If discouragement turns into constant anxiety or hopelessness, talk to a counselor, a doctor or a priest. If you ever think of harming yourself, you are not alone: please reach out right away to someone you trust or a crisis helpline. At “N.”, say the student’s name; if you are praying for yourself, say “me”.

      Praying for a student

      If you pray for a brother, sister, son, daughter or friend, pray with patience. Pressure and constant questions about deadlines rarely help. A meal, an offer to proofread, or simply asking “How can I help this week?” often does.

      The 3 Intercessors

      St. Thomas Aquinas

      c. 1225–1274 · Feast · Jan 28

      Patron of Catholic universities & schools

      Dominican friar who studied under St. Albert the Great and taught at the University of Paris. His Summa Theologiae works through questions, objections and replies. A Doctor of the Church.

      St. Albert the Great

      c. 1200–1280 · Feast · Nov 15

      Patron of natural scientists

      Dominican friar, bishop and teacher of St. Thomas Aquinas, who studied plants, animals and minerals firsthand. Declared a Doctor of the Church in 1931 and patron of natural scientists in 1941.

      St. Giuseppe Moscati

      1880–1927 · Feast · Nov 16

      Physician, researcher & teacher

      Physician in Naples who did laboratory research, taught at the university and cared for the poor, often without charge. He went to Mass daily. Canonized by Pope John Paul II in 1987.
