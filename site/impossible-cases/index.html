      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, You know the situation we bring before You, the one that feels tangled beyond fixing and heavier than we can carry. We have tried, we are tired, and some days we are close to losing hope. Yet we believe that nothing is impossible for You, and we ask for the grace to trust You whatever the outcome. Today we ask Our Lady, Undoer of Knots; St. Jude Thaddeus, faithful friend in hopeless need; and St. Rita of Cascia, steadfast in hope through every loss, to join their prayers with ours before Your throne, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        In My Distress

        The psalmist does not begin with praise or with a tidy request. He begins in distress, and he says so. That is where many of us stand as we begin this novena: a relationship that has broken, an illness that drags on, a case in court, a loved one far from God, a burden that will not lift. We do not have to feel hopeful before we pray. The first honest prayer is simply to tell God where we are, and ask Him to hear.

        The psalms give words to people who feel they have none. A cry of distress is already a seed of hope: we cry out only to someone we believe can hear. Many turn to St. Jude at the end of their rope. If the darkness brings thoughts of harming yourself, you are not alone: please reach out today to someone you trust or a crisis helpline.

      3. 3Pray

        Heavenly Father, in our distress we call upon You. You know this situation better than we do: how long it has lasted, how much it hurts, and how little we can change it by ourselves. We are tired, and some days we are close to giving up. Hear our voice. Through the prayers of St. Jude, faithful friend of those without hope, keep us from despair and help us take this one day at a time.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Our Lady, Undoer of Knots, pray for us.

        St. Jude Thaddeus, pray for us.

        St. Rita of Cascia, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, You hold every tangled thread of our lives. Loosen what binds us, give us light for the next step and peace while we wait, and let Your holy will be done. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

        Today’s step

        Write down, in a few honest sentences, the situation you are bringing to God, and keep the page where you pray these nine days.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention. If the day has a picture, take a moment to look at it; it is there to help you see the passage.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days. Seek wise counsel too: a priest, a trusted friend, and a doctor, counselor or lawyer where needed (Day 4). No novena guarantees a particular outcome, and there is nothing to copy, pass on or publish: simply pray and trust God. Steer clear of fortune-tellers, charms and anyone selling a sure fix. If you ever think of harming yourself, you are not alone: please reach out right away to someone you trust or a crisis helpline.

      The 2 Intercessors

      St. Jude Thaddeus

      1st century · Feast · Oct 28 (with St. Simon)

      Patron of desperate cases & lost causes

      One of the Twelve Apostles, called Thaddaeus by Matthew and Mark. Tradition holds that he preached the Gospel far from home and died a martyr. Christians have long turned to him when all seems lost.

      St. Rita of Cascia

      1381–1457 · Feast · May 22

      Patron of impossible causes

      Born near Cascia in Umbria, she was a wife, mother and widow before entering the Augustinian convent there. Venerated at Cascia since her death, she was canonized in 1900.
