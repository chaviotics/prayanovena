      1. 1Opening Prayer

        Pause for a moment of silence before God, then make the Sign of the Cross.

        In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

        Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Your faithful and kindle in them the fire of Your love.

        Heavenly Father, You invite us to share in Your work. As we prepare to serve, we bring You our nerves: the fear of a wrong note, a missed cue or a stumbled word. Remind us that this work is Yours before it is ours. Make us humble, ready and at peace, and use us, weak as we are, to draw Your people to You. Today we ask Mary, the Handmaid of the Lord; St. Martha, who served You in her home; St. Cecilia, who sang to You in her heart; and St. Thérèse, who offered You little things with great love, to join their prayers with ours, through Christ our Lord. Amen.

      2. 2Scripture & Reflection

        DAY 1

        Nervous Before We Serve

        Martha opened her home to Jesus and set to work, and the work swallowed her peace. Jesus did not scold her for serving. He named what was happening inside her: anxious and troubled about many things. Before a big event we know that feeling well: the setlist, the cues, the reading we keep rehearsing, the fear that something will go wrong in front of everyone. Jesus speaks our name too. One thing is needful: to be with Him first. So we begin these nine days at His feet.

        Martha is not the villain of this story. John’s Gospel tells us that Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus (John 11:5). When Lazarus died, it was Martha who went out to meet Jesus and said, “Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God” (John 11:27). Later, at a supper in Bethany, “Martha served” (John 12:2). Her serving never stopped; it found its center.

      3. 3Pray

        Lord Jesus, You looked at Martha with love and spoke her name, and You see us in our nerves today. We are anxious and troubled about many things: our part, our skills, the people watching. Through the prayers of St. Martha and of Mary, the Handmaid of the Lord, quiet our hearts. Teach us to sit at Your feet before we work, and to serve from the peace of being loved, not from the fear of failing.

        (Name your intention.) Amen.

      4. 4Closing Prayer

        Mary, Handmaid of the Lord, pray for us.

        St. Martha, pray for us.

        St. Cecilia, pray for us.

        St. Thérèse of Lisieux, pray for us.

        Lord Jesus, You came not to be served but to serve. Take our hands, our voices and our gifts, and our mistakes too. Work through us as You will, and let all the glory be Yours. Amen.

        Memorare

        Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone who fled to your protection, implored your help, or sought your intercession was left unaided. Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto you, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother. To you do I come; before you I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in your mercy hear and answer me. Amen.

      5. 5Pray together

        Our Father

        Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

        Hail Mary

        Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

        Glory Be

        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

        Close with the Sign of the Cross.

        Today’s step

        Write down your fears about your part, one per line. Read them to Jesus, then sit with Him in silence for three minutes.

      How to Pray This Novena

      1. Pause for a moment of silence before God.
      2. Pray the Opening Prayer, beginning with the Sign of the Cross.
      3. Read the day’s Scripture and reflection, then pray the day’s prayer, naming your intention.
      4. Pray the Closing Prayer and the Memorare.
      5. End with an Our Father, a Hail Mary and a Glory Be, and the Sign of the Cross. Then take today’s step with you.

      Praying with others

      One person leads each part; all respond pray for us and Amen, and say the closing prayers together. The prayers say “we”; if you pray alone, say “I”.

      If your event comes before Day 9

      A novena is a devotion, not a rule, so it is fine to start late. Begin with Day 1 today and pray as many days as you can before the event; finish the rest afterward as thanksgiving and prayer for the people who came. Short on time? Day 7 (mistakes) and Day 8 (the people who will come) fit the morning itself.

      Alongside prayer

      If you can, go to Confession and Mass before the event, and pray with your team before you begin, even for a minute. Prepare well, then rest: sleep is part of serving. If nerves ever become overwhelming, talk to your leader, a priest or someone you trust.

      The 3 Intercessors

      St. Martha

      1st century · Feast · Jul 29

      Patron of servants & hospitality

      Sister of Mary and Lazarus of Bethany, whom Jesus loved. She welcomed Him into her home and served, once anxious over many things, and later confessed Him as the Christ, the Son of God.

      St. Cecilia

      3rd century (tradition) · Feast · Nov 22

      Patron of musicians

      A Roman martyr named in the First Eucharistic Prayer of the Mass. An ancient account says that at her wedding, while the musicians played, she sang to the Lord alone in her heart.

      St. Thérèse of Lisieux

      1873–1897 · Feast · Oct 1

      Patron of missions

      A French Carmelite who died at 24. Her “little way” is doing small, hidden things with great love and trusting God like a child. She wrote and acted in plays for her community.
